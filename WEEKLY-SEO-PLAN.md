# Weekly SEO plan

Written 14 August 2026. The website side is largely done. What is left is the part
that needs a person doing jobs, taking photos and asking customers for reviews.

## First, why you are 11th

Your suburb pages went live on 14 August 2026. A `site:` search that day returned
only the old pages: the homepage, about, gallery, services, contact. **The new
pages were not in Google's index at all.**

So the 11th position was your homepage ranking for "electrician {suburb}", not
the dedicated suburb page. That page did not exist a week ago. Local pages
normally take 2 to 4 months to settle, longer where established competitors sit
above you.

Nothing is broken. The clock started this week.

Keep your name written exactly as **JPD Complete Electrical** everywhere,
including invoices, socials, and every directory listing.

## The one thing that matters most

For "electrician {suburb}", the map pack sits above the organic results and takes
most of the clicks. Map pack ranking is driven by proximity, relevance and
prominence. You cannot change proximity. Relevance is now handled on the website.

**Prominence is mostly reviews.** It is the single biggest lever you have and it
is entirely in your control.

## Weekly rhythm

### Every week, non-negotiable

**1. Ask every customer for a review.** Not "leave us a review if you like".
Send the link the same day, while the job is fresh, with a one-line message.
Target **2 to 3 a week**. At that rate you have 30 to 40 within three months,
which is genuinely competitive in this patch.

Ask them to mention the suburb and the job type where it is natural. "Justin
replaced our switchboard in Modbury" is worth several times "great service",
because it puts the suburb and the service in the review text.

**2. Reply to every review.** All of them, good and bad, within a few days.
Reply text is indexed and it signals an active listing. Work the suburb into the
reply naturally.

**3. Post one photo set to Google Business Profile.** Real job photos, taken on
the day. This is the most under-used ranking factor in the trades and it takes
two minutes.

**4. Send me one job write-up.** Rough notes plus five photos: what the property
was, what you found, what you did. I turn it into a `{Service} in {Suburb}` post.
That is the format Marcus has 60 of and it is the one thing you can produce that
he cannot copy, because they are your jobs.

Two of those a month is 24 a year, each carrying a service-plus-suburb keyword.

### Every fortnight

**5. One directory listing.** Name, address and phone written identically every
time. Work through: True Local, Yellow Pages, Hotfrog, Local Search, StartLocal,
Aussie Web, Word of Mouth, ServiceSeeking, Oneflare, the Tea Tree Gully Connect
directory (Mastin is listed there), NECA's member directory.

Consistency matters more than volume. One inconsistent listing does more harm
than three good ones do good.

### Every month

**6. Check Search Console.** Which queries are showing you, at what position,
and which pages are getting impressions but no clicks. That last group is usually
a title or description problem and is quick to fix.

**7. Ask two past customers for a Facebook recommendation.** Different platform,
same signal, and it feeds the brand searches that influence local ranking.

## Social media, since you asked

Social is not a direct ranking factor. Google does not count your follower count.
But it matters for two indirect reasons that are real:

- **Brand searches influence local ranking.** People who see your work and later
  search "JPD Complete Electrical" are a positive signal. Social is what causes
  that search.
- **The content is the same asset.** A job you photograph once becomes an
  Instagram post, a GBP photo post, and a blog job report. Film once, use three
  times. That is the only version of social media worth your time as a
  one-person business.

What actually works for trades, in rough order:

1. **Before and after switchboards.** The single best-performing trade content
   there is. An old ceramic fuse board next to a new labelled RCBO board is
   genuinely striking to a non-electrician.
2. **Short "why this is a problem" clips.** Point the camera at a scorched
   terminal or a melted outlet and explain it in twenty seconds. This is
   educational, it is shareable, and it demonstrates expertise.
3. **Thermal imaging footage.** You have a camera almost nobody else in your
   patch has. A thermal image of a hot connection is unusual and gets watched.
4. **The tidy-up.** Photograph the site clean at the end. Customers notice.

Post the same thing to Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. Do not write three
different captions. If you are choosing one to put effort into, Facebook is where
the Adelaide homeowner demographic that buys switchboard upgrades actually is;
TikTok reaches a younger renting audience who mostly are not your customer.

**Always geotag the suburb.**

## What I still need from you

figures, because inventing your numbers would be dishonest. Cost queries are high
intent and Marcus ranks on them. Ranges from your price book, framed as "from" or
"typically", would strengthen 14 pages at once.

**Job write-ups.** As above. This is the highest-value thing you can send me.

**Your ABN.** Mastin publishes theirs as a trust signal. Yours is not on the site.

**Search Console access, or confirmation the sitemap is submitted.**
`https://jpdcompleteelectrical.com.au/sitemap.xml`, 53 URLs, most of them new
this week. This is the fastest way to get them crawled and it is the single most
time-sensitive item on this page.
